## What was run

D13 (`PAPER_RATIFIED_2026_09_06`) rebalances the whole book on the first session of each month and reads the regime (NIFTY 50 TRI against its 200-session average) only then. The variants, each D13's own parameters plus M14.6 switches (`backtest/policies/momentum_v2.py`), research configurations only:

* **Momentum v2, D13 paper config** — the paper book's configuration (D13): M9.5's toggles without the 15% vol target.
* **V1 D13 + daily slot fill** — D13's monthly exits; each session a book short of 20 names buys the best-ranked unheld top-20 names into the free slots while the monthly regime reading is risk-on.
* **V2 per-stock 21-session clock** — no calendar rebalance: each holding is reviewed 21 sessions after its entry (sold outside the top-30, else kept and its clock restarted); regime read monthly, as D13.
* **V3 per-stock clock + daily regime gate** — the regime read every session; risk-off blocks entries and top-ups, sells nothing.
* **V3b per-stock clock + daily regime park** — as V3, and the first risk-off session parks the whole book (re-issued monthly).
* **V4 per-stock clock, no regime filter** — V2 with no regime filter: buy whenever a top-20 name is unheld and a slot is free.

Mandate (owner, 2026-09-07): rank on XIRR ÷ max drawdown; both liquidity floors (₹1 cr and ₹10 cr median daily turnover); the decade, the six-year window and the walk-forward (choose on 2016-09..2021-08, verify on 2021-09..2026-08), never averaged; bar: backtested XIRR > 25%. Floor-only universe (as M14.5: the PIT NIFTY 500 history opens 2016-10-24). Book corporate actions and interest on idle cash on; every order through A8's ratified rails and the one SimBroker/cost-model path.

## Analysis and recommendation (hand-written, 2026-10-14)

*Everything above the marker is generated from the saved runs; this section is written by hand
from those tables. Where it quotes a number, the number is in a table above.*

### The owner's question, answered first

> "We hold zero stocks, and every day one or other stock is potentially eligible to enter. The
> monthly rebalance should be per stock, not per 30 days."

**The paper book is empty because of the regime filter, not because of the cadence.** Run forward
from 2026-09-01 with the same rails, broker and cost model, every variant that keeps the regime
filter — D13, V1 (daily slot fill), V2 (per-stock clock), V3 (daily regime gate) and V3b (daily
regime park) — holds **zero names** after the 2026-10-13 fills, on both floors, because the
NIFTY 50 TRI read below its 200-session average on every session of the span (−7.28% on
2026-10-12). Changing *when* a slot may be filled changes nothing while the gate that admits entries
is shut. Only V4, which drops the regime filter, would hold a book today: 20 names at ₹1 cr
(ACUTAAS, SKYGOLD, AEROFLEX, THANGAMAYL, KMEW, RPTECH, ATHERENERG, KIRLOSENG, WELCORP, SHREEJISPG,
GRWRHITECH, TDPOWERSYS, CUPID, YASHO, SILVERTUC, SAKAR, RRKABEL, CPPLUS, RPEL, SANSERA) and 21 at
₹10 cr (one more than `top_n` — a review sale that did not fill leaves its replacement as an extra
name until the next review). Over these six weeks V4's book is up 7.1% (₹1 cr) / 11.1% (₹10 cr)
against 0.4% for cash; six weeks is not evidence either way, and the decade below is.

So the two effects the brief asked to separate do separate cleanly: **(1) per-stock timing** is
V1/V2 against D13, with the regime read exactly as D13 reads it; **(2) the regime gate** is V3/V3b
(read daily) and V4 (no gate) against V2.

### "Zero stocks" never happens in the backtest — but a lot of cash does

No variant's backtested book is ever empty after its first session (sessions with no name: 0.0–0.1%,
the opening day only). A8's 8-name minimum-holdings floor refuses the sells that would take a parked
book below eight names (M14.5 documents this), so a D13 park leaves 8–10 names. The live paper book
is empty only because it *started* on a risk-off session with nothing to keep.

What the backtest does show is **idle cash**: D13's decade book averages **32% cash at ₹1 cr and
25% at ₹10 cr**. That cash is parks (the regime read risk-off on 21% of decade sessions), buys A8's
35% sector cap refused (464 at ₹1 cr) whose slot then waits a month, and sale proceeds between
rebalances. Per-stock timing does put that cash to work — V1 28%/21%, V2 19%/15%, V3 12%/9%, V4
5%/2% — and the mean number of names held rises from ~18 to ~19–20. **But more exposure did not buy
more return per unit of drawdown.**

### Per-stock timing (V1, V2) against D13

* **V2 (21-session clock per holding) loses to D13 in 7 of 8 cells** on XIRR/DD and has the larger
  drawdown in 7 of 8: decade 0.77 vs 0.87 (₹1 cr), 0.56 vs 0.80 (₹10 cr); wf-verification 1.01 vs
  1.93 (₹1 cr). It trades on 58–94 sessions a year instead of ~17 and turns over up to 3.3x a year
  against D13's 2.0–2.7x, with 5–10x as many sector-cap refusals (a refused entrant is retried with
  the next name the following session). Its only better cell is wf-selection ₹1 cr. Reviewing each
  name on its own clock rather than all at once is not, on this evidence, an improvement.
* **V1 (D13's exits, free slots refilled every session) is the only variant with a case**: better
  XIRR/DD than D13 in **5 of 8 cells** (decade both floors: 0.93 vs 0.87, 0.88 vs 0.80; wf-selection
  both; wf-verification ₹10 cr: 1.64 vs 1.20). It is **not consistent**: it is worse in both six-year
  cells (1.03 vs 1.54 at ₹1 cr, 1.47 vs 1.56 at ₹10 cr) and much worse in the verification window at
  ₹1 cr (0.80 vs 1.93 — XIRR 24.2% vs 33.0%, drawdown 30.2% vs 17.1%). The walk-forward chose V1 on
  the selection window (₹1 cr) and V1 then lost to D13 on verification at that floor, while winning
  at ₹10 cr. Its charges run +2% to +36% over D13's. What V1 changes is narrow — it fills the slots
  D13 leaves empty between rebalances (mostly sector-cap refusals and names that left the universe)
  — and the drawdown increase at ₹1 cr after 2021 says those mid-month fills, at whatever ranks
  top-20 that session in the thinner names, are not free.

### The regime gate (V3, V3b, V4) against V2

* **V3 (regime read daily, risk-off blocks entries but sells nothing) loses to D13 in all 8 cells**
  and has the largest drawdowns of the gated variants (40.2% decade ₹1 cr, 52.2% at ₹10 cr): without
  D13's park, a book that is full when the index breaks down rides the whole bear leg; blocking new
  entries does not protect what is held.
* **V3b (daily park) loses in all 8 cells** too, with the most turnover of any arm (3.6–4.6x a year)
  and charges up to +93% — the whipsaw M14.5 measured for a daily exit, now on a per-stock book.
* **V4 (no regime filter) answers "every day some stock is eligible" literally**: it has the highest
  raw XIRR in the study (31.2% decade ₹1 cr, 36.3% six-year ₹1 cr) and the worst drawdowns (37–55%),
  so it **loses to D13 on XIRR/DD in all 8 cells** (0.40–0.83 against D13's 0.65–1.93). This is the
  regime filter's value, measured: it costs some return and roughly halves the drawdown.

### Against the >25% XIRR bar

D13 clears it in 5 of 8 cells, V1 in 5, V2 in 6, V3 in 4, V3b in 3, V4 in 5. No variant clears it
in every cell, and the ones that clear it more often do so by carrying more drawdown.

### Recommendation

1. **Do not move the paper book to a per-stock cadence (V2) or loosen the regime gate (V3, V3b,
   V4).** None beats D13 consistently; V3, V3b and V4 lose in every one of the eight cells, V2 in
   seven. The paper book's empty state is the regime filter doing what the backtest says it is
   for, and per-stock timing would not change it: under every gated variant the book would hold
   nothing today.
2. **V1 (daily slot fill) is the only candidate worth a further look, and it is not ready.** It
   improves XIRR/DD in 5 of 8 cells but fails the "all three windows, both floors" test, including
   the walk-forward verification at ₹1 cr, so it should not be ratified on this evidence. If it is
   pursued, the first thing to fix is its interaction with A8's sector cap (683 refusals on the
   decade at ₹1 cr against D13's 464), then re-run.
3. **If the owner's concern is exposure rather than cadence, the honest lever is the regime filter
   itself**, and the price of removing it is V4's 37–55% drawdowns. That is a ratification decision
   for the owner (M14.5 already found that reading the regime more often does not help either).

Changing the paper book's strategy is the owner's call; this report only recommends.

### Decisions taken in building the variants (documented, not tuned)

* **Ranking on a non-rebalance session** is the rebalance's own: the 12-1 return (close one month
  ago over close twelve months ago, L2-adjusted), the same universe screen and liquidity floor,
  ties by ISIN, recomputed from that session's point-in-time closes. A test proves the daily L1
  ranking reads no close after its session and that rewriting every later close leaves it unchanged.
* **Review clock**: 21 sessions (one trading month) from the session the entry was decided; a
  review sells outside the top-30 band, else keeps the name, tops it up toward its weight when it
  ranks within the top-20, and restarts its clock. The clock is also the minimum holding period —
  only a regime park sells a name earlier. A sale A8 refuses is retried at the next review, not daily.
* **Sizing**: an entrant's target is its inverse-volatility share of the book it completes (held
  names not being sold plus the entrants), times the book's value; buys are capped by free cash
  × 0.98 (pro rata) and A8's per-order ceiling. An entry that could not be completed from the cash
  free that day (proceeds settle T+1/T+2) is topped up on later sessions until its first review.
* **An entry that does not fill** (a rail refusal, no bar) puts that name on a one-review cooldown
  so the next-ranked name gets the slot.
* **V1's gate** is D13's: open unless the last monthly rebalance parked the book. **V2's** regime is
  read on the first session of each month exactly as D13 reads it (risk-off parks the book).
  **V3** reads it every session and only blocks entries and top-ups; **V3b** also parks the whole
  book on the first risk-off session, re-issuing the park on each month's first session while
  risk-off persists. **V4** never reads it.
* The switches are off by default and absent from the parameters' `repr` while off; D13's replay
  digests are byte-identical to M14.5's (table above) and a unit test pins D13's digest on a
  synthetic replay struck with the pre-M14.6 policy.
