## What was run

D13 (`PAPER_RATIFIED_2026_09_06`) reads the regime — NIFTY 50 TRI against its 200-session average — only on the monthly rebalance session. The variants (M14.5 switches on D13's own parameters, nothing else changed):

* **Momentum v2, D13 paper config** — the paper book's configuration (D13): M9.5's toggles without the 15% vol target.
* **D13 + daily regime re-entry** — monthly exit; a parked book re-enters the first session the index is >= its 200d MA.
* **D13 + daily regime re-entry and exit** — the regime read every session, both ways: park on risk-off, re-enter on risk-on.
* **D13 + daily regime re-entry, 2% band** — daily re-entry only once the index is >= 1.02 x its 200d MA (whipsaw damping).

Mandate (owner, 2026-09-07): rank on XIRR ÷ max drawdown; both liquidity floors (₹1 cr and ₹10 cr median daily turnover); the decade, the six-year window and the walk-forward (choose on 2016-09..2021-08, verify on 2021-09..2026-08), never averaged; bar: backtested XIRR > 25%. Book corporate actions and interest on idle cash on; every order through A8's ratified rails.

### The baseline is the baseline

D13 here is a fresh replay, not a copy, and it reproduces the saved M12.R campaign
(`~/campaign/m12-rerun-2bb2b08`) exactly in all eight floor-only cells: e.g. decade ₹1 cr
25.06% / 28.71%, ₹10 cr 24.14% / 30.05%; verification ₹1 cr 33.04% / 17.10%. The code check is
in the tests: D13's parameter `repr` (its run fingerprint) is unchanged by M14.5, and a replay
with the switches off reproduces, byte-for-byte, the journal, book and rails digest of the policy
before M14.5.

### Scorecard against D13 (XIRR ÷ max drawdown, 12 cells: 8 floor-only + 4 NIFTY 500)

| Variant | Beats D13 on XIRR/DD | Max DD worse than D13 | Parks over the decade (D13: 9) | Decade charges, ₹1 cr (D13: ₹2.20L) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daily re-entry (monthly exit) | **0 / 12** | 12 / 12 (+1.36 to +11.22pp) | 12 | ₹2.52L |
| Daily re-entry and exit | 1 / 12 | 10 / 12 (up to +11.97pp) | 39 | ₹3.13L |
| Daily re-entry, 2% band | 8 / 12 | 5 / 12 (up to +5.77pp) | 10 | ₹2.19L |

* **Daily re-entry, the variant the index proxy favoured, is rejected.** It never beats D13 and
  deepens the drawdown in every cell. The index-only proxy (9.47% / 15.02 DD vs 8.71 / 19.52)
  does not carry over to the stock book: re-entering on the first session the index regains its
  average buys a fresh top-20 of high-beta momentum names right at the crossings that mostly
  reverse (61% of crossings since 2016 reverse within 5 sessions), and with exits still monthly a
  failed re-entry is then held for up to a month.
* **Daily re-entry and exit is rejected.** It trades the regime about four times as often (39
  parks over the decade vs 9), with one-way turnover of 3.3–3.7× a year against 2.3–2.6× and
  about 40% more in charges. Exiting on the first breakdown does not cap the drawdown: decade at
  ₹10 cr is 42.03% against D13's 30.05%.
* **The 2% banded re-entry is the only variant with a case, and the case is not strong enough.**
  It wins 8 of 12 cells, including all four NIFTY 500 cells, and NIFTY 500 at ₹1 cr is the paper
  book's own universe (`UniverseParameters()` = `nifty500`, ₹1 cr floor): six-year 28.39% / 20.82%
  vs 26.65% / 21.57%; verification 26.97% / 19.85% vs 22.79% / 22.03%. But it loses where the
  mandate's selection is made. On the walk-forward selection window (2016-09..2021-08, ₹1 cr,
  chosen by code before verification is read), **the code chose D13** (0.85 vs 0.65 for the
  band), and on the ₹1 cr decade the band's drawdown is 34.48% against 28.71%. Both run from
  January 2018 to the 23 March 2020 low. The COVID leg is about the same for both (Feb–Apr 2020:
  17.5% vs 16.2%, from the saved NAV paths), so the extra depth built up during
  2018–2019 (the cause was not isolated here). Adopting it
  now would mean choosing on the verification window and the NIFTY 500 rows, which is the
  selection-on-test-data the mandate rules out.

### Against the >25% XIRR bar

D13 clears it on the ₹1 cr decade (25.06%), both six-year cells and both floor-only verification
cells. It misses on the ₹10 cr decade (24.14%), both selection cells and the NIFTY 500
verification cells. No variant changes that picture in its favour on the selection window.

### What actually limits the regime overlay: A8's 8-name floor (known; not changed here)

Every variant, D13 included, parks only part of the book. A8's minimum-holdings rail refuses any
sell that would take a book holding 8 or more names below 8, so a risk-off park sells the
basket down to 8 names and the rest stays invested through the risk-off spell. The table's last
column counts these refusals: 241 in D13's ₹1 cr decade run, 428 with daily exit. This was first
raised in `ops/gates/ai-analyst-plan-2026-09-07.md` ("`MIN_HOLDINGS=8` makes the regime gate
structurally unimplementable"). It is also why an index-only proxy, which goes fully to cash,
over-states what any regime-timing rule can do for this book. How a regime park should relate to
the floor (exempt a park from the rail, or accept a partial park) is an owner decision about the
ratified rails. M14.5 does not touch it.

### Recommendation

1. **Paper trading should not adopt any M14.5 variant.** Keep D13 as ratified. The daily
   re-entry and both-ways rules are rejected on every window. The 2% band failed the
   walk-forward selection that the mandate uses to decide.
2. **If the owner wants to test the band further**, make it a pre-registered candidate for
   the next selection test rather than adopting it on these numbers. That test should be a
   NIFTY 500 walk-forward, which becomes possible once M14.1's pre-2016-10-24 membership build
   is published to the lake (M14.1 verified the build but did not write the lake). Re-running is
   `uv run python -m backtest.m12_rerun --arm-set regime-daily --universe nifty500 --units
   walk-forward,decade …`.
3. **Settle the park-versus-floor question first.** Every regime-timing comparison here is
   measured on a book that keeps at least 8 names through risk-off. A rule for how a park meets
   the rail would change all four rows more than any of the daily switches does.
