# PE timing: is the market P/E below its own recent average a useful entry/exit signal? Study report, 2026-10-19

Pre-registration: `ops/studies/preregistration-pe-timing-2026-10-19.md`. It was committed at `a369952`
before any run and has not changed since; the render re-checks its sha256 against the frozen
baseline record. Runs: `~/campaign/pe-timing-a369952/` (not committed; `data/`-class artefacts).
Log: `~/campaign/pe-timing-a369952-2026-10-19.log`. Driver: `python -m backtest.pe_timing`.

## Answer

**No. Using the market P/E against its own monthly (21-session), quarterly (63-session) or
200-session average did not help, either as an overlay on Momentum v2 (D13) or as a standalone
index-timing rule.** All 18 D13 overlay arms are **REJECT** under the pre-registered rule at
₹10 crore/day. All 6 standalone timing arms are **REJECT** against buy-and-hold. Every arm lost
return. Some arms cut drawdown, but none by enough to make up for the lost return. D13 stays as the
paper configuration.

The main reason is that "P/E below its recent mean" is a **buy-the-dip signal**, not a trend
filter. It reads *cheap* after the market has fallen and *rich* after it has risen, which is close
to the opposite of what D13's price regime does. A momentum book wants to be invested in rising
markets, and the P/E gate keeps it out of them.

The two signals **disagree more often than chance**: agreement is 30–40 %, below the ~47 % that two
independent signals with these base rates (price on 80 % of months, P/E cheap ~45 %) would give. When D13's price regime says risk-off, the 200-session P/E gate says
*cheap* 96 % of the time. When the market has just fallen, the P/E reads cheap about 80 % of the
time. It is a dip-buying / mean-reversion signal, roughly the inverse of a trend filter. Combining
it with a trend book as an AND (A3) mostly keeps the book in cash (82–85 months of 120). Replacing
the trend filter with it (A2) buys falling markets, and drawdown worsens in F2 and F3.

### The methodology break

- **Found in the data, as NSE documented:** on **2021-03-31** the NIFTY 500 P/E fell 43.33 → 35.89
  (−17.2 %) on a −0.6 % index day, and the NIFTY 50 P/E fell 40.43 → 33.20 (−17.9 %). This is the
  standalone → consolidated earnings change. On NIFTY 50 it is the largest one-session move in
  implied earnings in the whole 2012–2026 series. On NIFTY 500 it is the largest except for a
  one-day bad print on 2019-08-05 (23.49 between 28.91 and 28.84), which reverts the next day,
  affects no rebalance read, and was not corrected.
- Two smaller one-session steps were not spliced, because neither is an identified basis change:
  2019-09-25 (−9 %, both indices) and 2020-09-25 (+10.7 %, NIFTY 500 only).
- **Splice:** pre-break P/Es are restated by k = **0.833286** (the implied-earnings ratio across
  the break). This is applied only to readings on or after 2021-03-31, so it is point-in-time.
- **Effect on the rule:** on D13's monthly reads the raw gate fired spuriously *cheap* on
  2021-04-01 (21d) and on 2021-04-01 and 2021-05-03 (200d). It made no difference at 63d. So the
  break mattered less than feared at a monthly cadence. It moves F2 by 1.7–5 pp for the affected
  arms, and in every case the spliced arm is no better than the raw one. Neither series changes
  any verdict.

## Recommendation

- **Do not add a P/E-vs-trailing-mean gate to D13**, in any of the three forms. Do not use it as a
  standalone index-timing rule either. Every form lowered return. The rule is a dip-buying signal
  and it fights the momentum book's trend filter.
- If the owner's real concern is drawdown, the evidence points elsewhere. D13's own price regime
  already does most of the drawdown work. The one P/E arm that cut drawdown sharply (price AND
  P/E<63d: 16.6 % vs 29.9 %) did it by staying in cash 70 % of the time and paid about 6 pp a year
  for it.
- Paper keeps running D13 unchanged.

## Follow-ups (new trials, not run)

- **P/E as a level, not a trend:** for example, P/E percentile against a long (5–10 year) history,
  read only at extremes. This is a valuation signal rather than a 1–10 month mean-reversion signal,
  and it would need its own pre-registration. The 2021 break matters much more for long windows.
- **"Price AND P/E<63d" as a drawdown overlay** judged under a drawdown-first rule. That is a
  different decision rule and therefore a new trial.
- **Earnings-yield vs bond-yield gap** (needs a G-sec series the lake does not yet hold).

## Part B — standalone index timing on the NIFTY 500 TRI (generated)

References (not trials): buy-and-hold, and the same timing on the index's own 200-session average.
